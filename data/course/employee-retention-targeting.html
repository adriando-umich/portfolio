Assumptions: to replace an employee costs $25,000. A retention offer costs $10,000 and keeps 60% of the employees who would leave. An offer pays when the risk is above 67%.

Three groups hold almost all the leavers, and each has a different cause

Low load, low rating944 employees88%leave
Strong performers at 5 to 6 years514 employees85%leave
Overloaded1,521 employees40%leave
All other employees9,012 employees1%leave

Low load, low rating: 2 projects or fewer, low satisfaction and a low evaluation. Overloaded: 6 projects or more, or 270 hours a month or more. Strong performers: 5 to 6 years of tenure with a high evaluation and high satisfaction.

The model

Decision

Which employees get a retention offer.

Objective

The highest saving: replacement cost avoided, less the cost of the offers.

Method

Logistic regression and a random forest in R on 11,991 employee profiles: 70% to train, 30% to test.

  • Risk is not a straight line: employees with too little work and employees with too much work both leave. This is why the main-effects model fails (AUC 0.83) and the forest does not (AUC 0.99).
  • One offer does not fit all three groups. The overloaded group needs less work, not a bonus. Outside the three groups, only 1% of employees leave.
  • The file has 14,999 rows but only 11,991 different profiles. The repeats were removed before the split, so that the same profile is not in the training and the test data.
  • The saving depends on the three assumptions. With a lower success rate of the offer, the cutoff goes up and fewer employees get an offer.
