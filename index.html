Credit case: approval cutoff

Lending Club public loan data. Python, SQL, Excel.

Q: Can a new approval cutoff increase approvals at the same bad rate?

Yes. A scorecard cutoff approves more applicants at the same bad rate and adds $7.1M of realized NPV on the 2015 loans.

Why? Three reasons, strongest first

  1. It approves more applicants with no more defaults

    Approval rate: 85.7% vs. 84.5% today. Bad rate (default rate of approved loans): 12.5%, held equal.

  2. The gain holds under every cost assumption tested

    Realized NPV against the current policy: +$7.1M. Lowest result in the stress test: +$5.1M.

  3. A small test limits the risk of the change

    Start on 10% of applications. Stop if 12-month defaults rise 1 point.

The 2015 loans lost money under the current policy; the new cutoff recovers $7.1M

Fraud case: transaction policy

IEEE-CIS public transaction data. Python, SQL.

Q: Which policy gives the lowest total cost of fraud, false declines and reviews?

The lowest-cost policy cuts the total monthly cost by $64k and keeps false declines under the 3% cap.

Why? Three reasons, strongest first

  1. It lowers the cost that matters, not only fraud loss

    Total monthly cost = fraud loss that gets through + cost of false declines + cost of reviews. Decrease: $64,274 per month.

  2. The saving is the low end of the estimate

    The decrease is $64k to $127k per month, depending on the baseline. I report the lowest figure.

  3. Good customers stay protected

    False decline rate (share of good transactions declined): 2.46%, under the cap of 3%.

Marketing case: offer targeting

Hillstrom public email test data. R.

Q: Which customers do we send the offer to, for the best return?

Send the men's email to all customers and keep a 10% holdout. It earns $13k per campaign of 100,000 customers.

Why? Three reasons, strongest first

  1. The email raises spend

    Spend effect against the no-email group: +$0.77 per customer in the 2 weeks after the email.

  2. The extra margin is larger than the contact cost

    30% margin on $0.77, less $0.10 for each email, gives $13,095 of profit per 100,000 customers.

  3. A targeting model adds nothing at this cost

    At $0.10 per email, no model-picked segment earns more than an email to all customers.

Each case goes from a business question to a decision in four steps

  1. State the business question

    One question that a business leader must decide, with the result today and the result wanted.

  2. Compare the new policy with the current one

    Both policies run on the same data, so the difference comes from the policy alone.

  3. Put the effect in dollars

    Each result is a dollar figure, tested again under less favorable assumptions.

  4. Recommend, with a test and a stop rule

    The recommendation starts small, names the guardrail and says when to stop.
