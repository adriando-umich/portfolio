Situation

A lender approves 36-month personal loans by risk sub-grade. The loans from 2013–2014 made $71.7M of realized NPV.

Complication

The loans from 2015 lost $32.9M. Defaults rose from 12.3% in 2013 to 14.9%, and the mean interest rate fell from 13.4% to 11.3%. The business wants more approvals, but Risk accepts no more defaults.

Question

Can a new score approve more applicants at the current bad rate of 12.5%, and add money?

Yes. A scorecard cutoff approves more applicants at the same bad rate and adds $7.1M of realized NPV on the 2015 loans.

Why? Three reasons, strongest first

  1. It approves more applicants with no more defaults

    • Approval rate: 85.7% of the 2015 applicants vs. 84.5% today.
    • Bad rate (default rate of approved loans): 12.5% for both policies.
    • At the same number of approvals it swaps 24,426 loans. The loans it adds default less: 23.6% vs. 25.1%.

  2. The gain holds under every cost assumption tested

    • Realized NPV against the current policy, 2015 loans: +$7.1M.
    • Lowest result when cost of funds and acquisition cost change: +$5.1M.
    • The swapped loans alone add $8.3M.

  3. A small test limits the risk of the change

    • Start on a random 10% of applications.
    • Stop if the 12-month default rate is more than 1 point above the current policy.
    • About 1,000 random approvals below the cutoff measure the applicants that the data does not contain.
