Whether more listings help or hurt hosts in a vacation rental market

Course project, Michigan Ross MBA. Instrumental-variable regression (two-stage least squares) in R on 281 local markets. Not client or employer work.

R
R scriptTwo-stage least squares with robust standard errors
SituationA vacation rental marketplace operates in 281 local markets. Its growth teams add listings to make each market larger.
ComplicationMore listings can help hosts (more travelers come) or hurt them (more competition). A plain comparison of markets cannot tell, because hosts enter the markets where demand is already good.
QuestionWhat is the effect of more listings on the bookings of a host?
AnswerThey hurt. 10% more listings cut the booking confirmation rate by 1.7 points. A plain regression shows no effect.

Standard errors are robust to heteroskedasticity. Controls: inquiries per day, average number of rooms, share of apartments, share of executive listings, share near adventure sites and national parks.

The model

Question

Does a larger market bring each host more bookings (more travelers come) or fewer (more hosts compete)?

Problem

Listings are not random. Hosts enter the markets where demand is already good, so a plain regression mixes the two directions.

Method

Two-stage least squares. New listings from a migration of hosts move the market size for a reason that is not local demand.

  • The occupancy effect has the same sign (-0.25 points) but is not statistically clear (p = 0.13).
  • The first-stage F statistic is 12.8. The instrument is usable, but the estimates are not precise.
  • For the marketplace, the result points to demand: more travelers, not more hosts, are what the weak markets need.
  • The instrument is valid only if the new listings affect confirmations through market size alone. The data cannot test this.
