Offer targeting for an email campaign

Situation

A retailer sent an email to customers who bought in the last year. Each customer went at random into one of three groups: the men's email, the women's email or no email. Visits, conversions and spend were measured for 2 weeks.

Complication

Each email has a contact cost. Most customers buy nothing in the test window, so the spend data is noisy, and the mean effect can hide customers where the email loses money.

Question

Which email version do we send, and to which customers, for the most incremental profit?

Send the men's email to all customers and keep a 10% holdout. It earns $13k per campaign of 100,000 customers.

Why? Three reasons, strongest first

  1. The email raises spend

    • Spend effect against the no-email group: +$0.77 for each customer in 2 weeks (95% CI $0.49 to $1.05).
    • The three groups differ only by the email: the randomization check passes.
    • The men's email gives $10,362 more than the women's email for each campaign.

  2. The extra margin is larger than the contact cost

    • 30% margin on $0.77, less $0.10 for each email: $13,095 for each 100,000 customers (95% CI $4,554 to $21,635).
    • The email pays at any contact cost below $0.23.

  3. A targeting model adds nothing at this cost

    • At $0.10 an email, no targeting rule and no uplift model beats an email to all customers.
    • A shorter list (the top 25% by purchase history) pays only if the full contact cost is $0.25 or more.
    • A 10% holdout confirms each campaign and costs $1,309.

Recommendation

Actions

  2. Keep a 10% holdoutcosts $1,309 a campaign, 87% power
