Which customers to call in a bank telemarketing campaign

Call the 16% of customers with a sale probability of 17% or more. Profit is twice the profit at the usual 50% cutoff, and a plain logistic regression beats six other models.

Course project, Michigan Ross MBA. Seven classifiers in R, including a stacked model, each with a call cutoff chosen for profit. Not client or employer work.

A sale earns $5 net and a call with no sale costs $1, so a call pays when the probability of a sale is above 1 in 6 (17%). Each cutoff was chosen on validation data and applied once to the test customers.

The model

Decision

Call or do not call each customer, from data that the bank has before the call.

Objective

The highest profit: $5 for each sale, less $1 for each call with no sale.

Method

Six classifiers and a stacked model. Data split: 50% to train, 25% to choose the cutoffs and fit the stacked model, 25% to test.

  • The plain logistic regression has the highest profit. In 2,000 resamples of the test customers, it is ahead of each other model more than 99% of the time.
  • The stacked model does not add profit here. The six models make similar errors, so there is little for a second level to correct.
  • The cutoff of the logistic regression (17%) is the same as the break-even probability. Its probabilities are well calibrated; those of the SVM are not.
  • Call duration is not a model input. It is known only after the call.
