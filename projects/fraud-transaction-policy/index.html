Situation

An online merchant accepts card payments. A set of fixed rules approves, reviews or declines each transaction.

Complication

Fraud loss goes up. Stricter rules decline more good customers, and each false decline costs margin and goodwill. The analyst team can examine only 120 alerts each day, and fixed rules lose value as fraud patterns change.

Question

Which policy gives the lowest total cost of fraud loss, false declines and reviews, with the same review capacity?

A three-tier policy (approve, review, decline) on a fraud score cuts the total monthly cost by at least $64k, with false declines inside the 3% limit.

Why? Three reasons, strongest first

  1. It stops almost twice the fraud dollars

    • Share of fraud dollars stopped: 71.2% vs. 38.4% for the current rules.
    • The score ranks fraud better than the rules: PR-AUC 0.486 vs. 0.269.

  2. The total cost falls under every comparison

    • Total cost each month: $172,612 vs. $299,432 for the base current policy (down 42%).
    • Against the strongest current policy tested, the decrease is $64,274 each month.
    • The decrease is at least $53,544 under every cost assumption tested.

  3. False declines rise, but stay inside the limit

    • Good transactions declined: 2.46% vs. 0.28% today. Limit: 3%.
    • Finance and Marketing confirm the cost of a false decline and the limit before the test.
    • The test runs on a random 10% of transactions for about 16 days, with stop rules.
