What is real data, and what is an assumption

The locations, the distances and the population are real. The transaction volumes, the transaction mix and the costs are assumptions, because a bank does not publish them. The results show a rule and its sensitivity. They do not show the real economics of a named site.

The model: value lost if the ATM is removed, minus the cost avoided

For each location, the model calculates the 5-year NPV of the owned ATM at a 10% discount rate. Volume falls 3.2% each year, the rate of US ATM withdrawals from 2021 to 2024.

Volume for each location is the base volume of its type multiplied by a volume index. The index is the residents in 2 miles divided by the mean of the type, limited to 0.5 to 1.5. Residents are a proxy. The index does not see workers, students or hospital visitors.

The access limit stops the rule at 8 of the 34 locations below break-even

Access is the share of residents who have a Capital One deposit ATM in 2 miles of the centre of their census tract. The base is 6.22 million residents in 5 miles of a Capital One ATM. The rule removes the location with the lowest NPV first, and stops when the next removal would decrease access by more than 1 point.

Next step: a test of offers on the ATM screen

The model sets the value of offers on the ATM screen to zero, because no public data measures it. A test can measure it: show an offer at a random half of the locations, keep the other half as control, and compare the number of new primary relationships. The 26 protected locations are the first place to run it, because more volume or more relationships there change the economics with no loss of access.
