How much to bid in a sealed auction when a win needs a later approval

Course project, Michigan Ross MBA. Decision tree with a win probability that depends on the bid, in Excel. Not client or employer work.

Excel
Excel modelDecision tree and the table of bids
SituationA developer plans a sealed bid of $5M for a property. If the project goes ahead, it is worth $15M for $8M of capital.
ComplicationA higher bid wins more often but leaves less profit. A win is also not the end: the plan then needs an approval that has a 30% chance, and without it the developer loses 10% of the bid.
QuestionWhich bid gives the highest expected value?
AnswerBid $4.11M, not the planned $5M. The expected value rises 35%, but the bid wins only 12% of the time.

The bid moves two things in opposite directions: the chance to win and the profit of a win

The model

Decision

The amount of one sealed bid for a property.

Objective

The highest expected value of the project.

Uncertainty

Two events in sequence: the bid wins or loses, then the plan is approved or not. The chance to win goes up with the bid.

  • With a fixed 20% chance to win, a $5M bid is worth $50k. When the chance to win depends on the bid, a lower bid of $4.11M is worth 35% more.
  • A $5M bid breaks even at an approval chance of 20%. The estimate is 30%, so the margin for error is 10 points.
  • The expected value is small against the stakes: $67k against a possible loss of $411k. A bidder who cannot take that loss must not bid.
  • The win curve is an assumption. The first thing to test is how the answer moves when the curve moves.
